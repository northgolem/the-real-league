Norwich, QPR and a title race hiding inside 1992/93

Remove today's Big Six and every match involving them. Norwich finish one point clear of QPR. The interesting part is what they both lose.

Norwich City finished the real 1992/93 season third, with 72 points and a goal difference of minus four. That is already an excellent conversation starter. Our version gives them another.

We remove Arsenal, Chelsea, Liverpool, Manchester City, Manchester United and Tottenham Hotspur. These are today's familiar Big Six names applied retrospectively, not a claim about who dominated English football in 1993. Every fixture involving any of those clubs disappears for both teams.

What remains is a 16-club table built from 240 actual matches. Norwich lead on 55 points. QPR have 54. Blackburn have 51, with Aston Villa fourth on 49.

Simply crossing out six names in the real table would leave Villa above Norwich. Recalculating the fixtures changes that. Villa lose 25 points earned against the excluded clubs; Norwich lose 17. QPR lose only nine. The same rule takes a very different bite out of each season.

Norwich and QPR each win 16 of their 30 remaining games. Norwich draw seven to QPR's six. One additional draw is the entire margin. QPR's superior goal difference cannot help them: points come first.

Further down, Crystal Palace offer the sharpest change of perspective. They were 20th in the published table. Here they are fifth on 44 points. Just five of their original 49 points came against the six excluded clubs. Among the surviving clubs, Palace move from 14th to fifth; that nine-place rise separates the effect of recalculation from the automatic promotion caused by deleting six names.

None of this awards Norwich a real championship or rescues Palace from their actual season. Different opponents, incentives and schedules would have produced a different football season. We have not simulated those changes. We have kept one specific set of results and counted it honestly.

Norwich still get the top row. The open-top bus remains outside our remit.
