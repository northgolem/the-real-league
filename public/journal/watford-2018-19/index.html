THE USURPER JOURNAL · 13 November 2026 · The Usurper League

Watford finished eleventh. Remove the Big Six and they finish first.

The 2018/19 table has an unexpected winner hiding in its lower half. The explanation is less mysterious than the headline.

Eleventh place does not normally require an open-top bus. Watford finished the 2018/19 Premier League season with 50 points, a negative goal difference and ten clubs above them. A perfectly respectable season. Not an obvious title story.

Yet remove the Big Six and every league fixture involving them, then calculate the table again, and Watford finish first. Not level on points, not rescued by an obscure tiebreaker. Five points clear.

The important word is “fixtures”. Simply crossing six names out of the real table would leave Wolves on top, followed by Everton, Leicester, West Ham and Watford. Watford would be fifth among the surviving clubs. To understand how they become first, we need to look at where those clubs earned their points.

The three points that disappear

Watford took just three league points from their twelve games against Arsenal, Chelsea, Liverpool, Manchester City, Manchester United and Tottenham. Their 2–1 home victory over Spurs on 2 September was the exception. The other eleven matches ended in defeat.

Remove that entire set of fixtures and only three of Watford’s 50 points disappear. Their record against the other thirteen clubs remains: thirteen wins, eight draws and five defeats. That is 47 points from 26 games, with 44 goals scored and 31 conceded.

Watford keep 94 per cent of their points while losing almost a third of their fixtures. That is the engine of this particular transformation. We have not awarded them a single extra point. We have removed matches that contributed almost none.

Their competitors have more to lose. Everton fall from 54 points to 42. Newcastle finish on 42 too, behind Everton on goal difference. Crystal Palace, West Ham, Leicester and Wolves all end on 41.

Wolves reveal the other side

Wolves were seventh in the real league, the highest-finishing club outside the six we remove. They earned 57 points, seven more than Watford.

But sixteen of those points came against the excluded clubs. Take those fixtures away and Wolves have 41. Watford, who began seven points behind them, now finish six points ahead.

That is not evidence that Wolves were overrated. Their results against those opponents were part of what made their real season good. It is evidence that “best of the rest” can mean two different things: the best overall campaign by a club outside the Big Six, or the best record in matches played solely between those clubs.

In 2018/19, those questions produce different answers.

A different table, not a stolen trophy

Manchester City remain the real champions. This calculation cannot tell us what would have happened if the Big Six had genuinely vanished before the season. Different schedules, incentives, squads and decisions would have produced a different competition. We keep the results that happened; we do not pretend to predict the matches that might have happened instead.

There is also a cost to our own rule. Watford’s win over Tottenham disappears alongside their eleven defeats. We cannot keep the enjoyable exception and discard everything else. Every fixture involving an excluded club goes, for both teams.

What survives is a complete 26-game record against the other remaining clubs. In that record, Watford stand alone at the top.

Eleventh in the Premier League. First in this particular alternative. The same season, viewed through a very different set of results.
