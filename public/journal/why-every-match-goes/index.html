Why removing a club means removing every match

Crossing a name out of a league table is easy. It leaves the wrong results behind.

If you remove Manchester United but leave every other club's points untouched, those clubs still carry the rewards and damage of having played United. The supposedly absent team is still influencing the table.

The Usurper League takes a stricter approach. When a club goes, every match involving it goes too. Both sides lose that match from their records: appearances, wins, draws, defeats, goals and points.

Take Norwich's 4–2 win at Arsenal in the opening round of 1992/93. If Arsenal are excluded, Norwich lose that appearance, that win, four goals scored, two conceded and three points. We do not preserve it because it was a good afternoon for Norwich.

The rule also removes defeats. Norwich's 3–1 defeat at Manchester City disappears if City are excluded. Norwich recover no points from that deletion, because they earned none. Their goals and matches played still change.

Apply the rule to the whole fixture list, then rebuild the table from the matches left. If two excluded clubs played one another, that fixture is removed once. It does not count as two missing games in the competition total.

That is why our 1992/93 calculation keeps 240 of 462 fixtures after excluding the six named clubs. Every one of the 16 remaining teams has 30 matches: home and away against the other 15.

It also explains why climbing the table does not mean gaining points. Crystal Palace finish fifth in that alternative table with 44 points, fewer than their real-season 49. They rise because the other surviving teams lose different amounts.

Historical scoring matters. The calculation must use the points awarded for a win in the season concerned, rather than imposing today's convention on every old campaign. Our alternative ordering uses points, goal difference and goals scored. Official head-to-head rules and historical sanctions are not fully reproduced in the hypothetical calculation; the published final table is a separate reference.

There is a limit to what the result tells us. Footballers would behave differently in a different competition. Clubs might spend differently. Managers might pick different teams. We do not have evidence for those imagined matches, so we do not invent them.

This is a way to inspect real results through a different selection of fixtures. You can disagree with the selection. You should be able to reproduce the arithmetic.
